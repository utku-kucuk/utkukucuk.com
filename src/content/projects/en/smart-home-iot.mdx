## The idea

I wanted my home automation to be **local, private and hackable** — no vendor cloud, no subscription, and hardware I understand down to the GPIO pin.

## What I built

- **Smart blind.** An ESP32 drives a NEMA17 stepper motor through an A4988 driver at 1/8 microstepping. Upper and lower limit switches stop the motor safely, physical buttons give manual open / close / stop, and the firmware adds cooling pauses on long runs to protect the motor.
- **Smart lamp.** An LED lamp with touchscreen control, colour effects and MQTT integration.
- **Climate sensors.** DHT22-based monitors for room temperature and humidity — including one inside 3D-printer filament boxes running on a LiPo battery.

## How it fits together

Every device is configured with ESPHome (with custom C/C++ where needed) and talks to the home server over **MQTT**. Home Assistant runs in **Docker on a Linux box**, exposing each device as entities for dashboards and automations.

## Why it's here

It's the same discipline as backend work at a smaller scale: message-driven integration, failure modes you have to design for, and a server you operate yourself.

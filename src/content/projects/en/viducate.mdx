## The problem

Lecture videos are large — often several gigabytes — and institutions want their staff to sign in with the accounts they already have. The platform had to accept big uploads reliably, play them back smoothly on ordinary connections and fit into a corporate directory.

## What I built

I designed the backend and the **video delivery** of Viducate, a platform for structured video courses, quizzes and progress tracking.

- **Uploads & processing.** Teachers upload lectures in chunks through a validated upload endpoint with configurable size limits; Nginx is tuned for multi-GB request bodies. FFmpeg reads durations and transcodes each video to 360p–1080p renditions without upscaling, stored with the rest of the metadata in PostgreSQL.
- **Streaming that doesn't freeze.** Videos are served with HTTP **byte-range** support (`Accept-Ranges`), so players can seek and buffer large files instead of downloading them whole.
- **Enterprise sign-in.** JWT authentication with three roles — **Admin**, **Teacher** and **Student** — plus **LDAP / Active Directory** login: local accounts are checked first, then the directory, and first-time corporate users are created just in time.
- **Running a school.** Admins bulk-import users from CSV with emailed credentials; students take quizzes and track progress per video.

## Running it

The API sits behind **Nginx** as a TLS-terminating reverse proxy on Linux, with Helmet, rate limiting and request validation in front of every route. The app runs under PM2, set up by a deployment script and template.

## What I'd improve

Package the renditions as HLS on a queue-backed worker pool for adaptive bitrate, move video files to object storage behind a CDN, and containerise the stack with Docker.
